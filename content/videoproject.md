# Math 105L: Video Project

One of your major pieces of work for the semester will be to create a video explaining a mathematical concept, either one from our class, or any other bit of math you find interesting or are curious about. This sheet outlines the project, the timeline for it, its structure, and its assessment.

## Organization {#organization .unnumbered}

You will work in assigned groups of two or three (most groups will be three). All students must appear in the video, and all students must do approximately equal work on topic selection and the storyboard/script (see below). Your video can be longish (up to five minutes) or short - the key is to clearly explain a concept! My suggestion would be to use TikTok. However, you may use any software you like.

## Content {#content .unnumbered}

The video you write, storyboard, and produce will introduce or explain a mathematical concept to a calculus student audience. You may pick
a calculus topic or something outside calculus. Some ideas are:

| 105L Concepts                              | Others                                      |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| What is a Limit?                           | Zeno's Paradoxes                            |
| Finding Tangent Lines                      | Fractals                                    |
| Using Log Rules to Solve Equations         | Tessellations                               |
| Continuity and Differentiability           | The Möbius strip and Klein Bottle           |
| Using the Product and Quotient Rules       | Non-Euclidean Geometry                      |
| The Chain Rule                             | The history of zero                         |
| Transforming Functions                     | The Abacus                                  |
| What does the slope of a curve mean?       | The Fibonacci Sequence and the Golden Ratio |
| What concavity tells us about human growth | Hotel Infinity                              |
| Proving the Power Rule                     |                                             |

You do not have to choose from this list – any mathematical topic you come up with may work! Just check your idea with me!

Your video can be serious or silly. It can be spoken or sung. It can use a whiteboard or paper, or take place outside. It can be about how **you** understood a concept that started off being really hard, or explain another interesting bit of math.

## Structure {#structure .unnumbered}

You will produce two major bits of work for the project: A [storyboard](https://www.techsmith.com/blog/video-storyboard/) and script (click that link to find out what a storyboard is!); and the video itself.

## Timeline {#timeline .unnumbered}

-   **You will need to meet and research a topic by Monday, Oct 11 at 8am**. By that time, your group should send me an email with your choice of
    topic and a list of resources you plan to use to learn about it. I will reply within a day or two, either asking for a bit more detail (or more resources), or approving your decision.
-   **The storyboard and script for your video will be due Monday, Nov 1 at 8am on Gradescope**. I would be more than happy to meet to discuss your work once in between these dates. It would be a good idea for your group to plan to have such a meeting with me. I will grade and return the storyboard and script to you within a week. **I may ask you to make revisions. If so, those will be due Monday, Nov 15** There will be a project workday on Friday, Nov 12.
-   **Your final video will be presented to the class during the last week of classes (Nov 29-Dec 3rd)**, the week after Thanksgiving. Students will sign up for time slots during which the video will be shown to your class.

## Assessment {#assessment .unnumbered}

You will need to pass both the storyboard and script assignment and the final video assignment in order to getting a passing grade for this element of the class:

-   **Storyboard** Your storyboard must include a slide for each scene of your video, explaining what will be seen and heard.
    
-   **Script** Your script should include all text spoken (or sung!) for each scene. The script must clearly explain the mathematical concept you choose. 50% of the script must pertain directly to the mathematical concept you choose. The script must be mathematically accurate.
    
-   **Video** You will get judged on the audio/visual clarity and quality; group participation (all members must be participating); and matching the script/storyboard.
