# Gradescope Submissions, Returns, and Resubmissions

All work for 105L will be submitted on [Gradescope](https://www.gradescope.com/courses/282393). Here are some guidelines, links, and instructions for submission:

* Once you have completed your work, you need to scan it to a PDF. You should use a document scanner app on your phone: [Evernote Scannable on iOS (iPhone/iPad)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/evernote-scannable/id883338188), or [Genius scan on Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.thegrizzlylabs.geniusscan.free&hl=en_US&gl=US). Instructions for creating a PDF using these [can be found here](https://help.gradescope.com/article/0chl25eed3-student-scan-mobile-device).

* When logging into Gradescope, be sure to use the 'School Credentials' button: 
  ![login screen with school button highlighted](/gradescopelogin.png)
  
  (You can instead log into Gradescope via [Sakai](https://sakai.duke.edu/portal/site/105lfall21) if you wish! That will log you in automatically.)

* After you've logged in, find the correct assignment in the Assignments area, and follow the [instructions here](https://help.gradescope.com/article/ccbpppziu9-student-submit-work#submitting_a_pdf). Most of the assignments in 105L will require you to assign questions to pages. If you do not do this, your work will not be graded until you fix it.

* For initial submissions, graders will assign 'correct', 'needs work', or 'missing' to each question. They will also comment on your work. No points will be assigned. You will then use their comments to correct your work and resubmit. 

* Each assignment will have a separate resubmit Gradescope assignment:
  ![login screen with school button highlighted](/gradescopesubresub.png)

* After you resubmit, graders will grade each question out of one point. Once those are returned, you will get a 'pass' or 'no-pass' on each assignment. You will get an email from me if you did not pass an assignment. Pass/no-passes will also be recorded in the Gradebook on [Sakai]( https://sakai.duke.edu/portal/site/105lfall21).

# Getting Help

You will need help with this class! Everyone will. Math at Duke is challenging, and this class will ask a lot of you, regardless of your math background. There are many resources available for help. Here's an outline with links:

* [**Help Room**](https://math.duke.edu/undergraduate/help-tutoring/help-rooms): Located in the Classroom building, the help room is open around 40 hours a week from 1-10pm M-Th and 4-10pm Su. It is staffed by TAs, graders, and others. You can go at any time to get help with your work. You can go do homework there and get help when you need it. It's a great space to have quiet work time, but have help readily available.
* **Office Hours**: I will have online office hours four hours per week ([see hours above](#class-information)). Here's a [Zoom link](https://duke.zoom.us/j/96644572829) for them.
* **Individual meetings**: I'm available to meet one on one by Zoom if you feel that's needed. Drop me an [email](mailto:rann@math.duke.edu)! I'm also available for quicker questions before/after class.
* [**EdStem Discussion Site**](https://edstem.org/us/courses/8577): You can post questions on the site from any worksheet or lab. The instructional team for the class will try to to answer them promptly. This is a great way to get pretty quick answers to your questions. You can post anonymously if you wish. If you have a question, many others are likely to have the same one! So go ahead and post! (Note: you may need to access the discussion board via [Sakai](https://sakai.duke.edu/portal/site/105lfall21).)
* [**The Academic Resource Center**](https://arc.duke.edu/): Duke's ARC provides multiple layers of support for classes. From peer tutoring to study groups, this is one of the most useful resources Duke provides. Make an appointment with an advisor there, or sign up for a study group!
